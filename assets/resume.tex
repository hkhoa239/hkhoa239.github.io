% ---------- Research Profile ----------
\section{Research Profile}

Honors Computer Science graduate from Ho Chi Minh City University of Technology
(HCMUT -- VNU-HCM), with research experience spanning
\textbf{computer vision, foundation models, and embodied AI}.
My current research direction is centered on
\textbf{vision--language--action (VLA) models and robotic learning},
particularly long-horizon manipulation, memory-augmented policies,
learning from human video, and scalable evaluation of embodied agents.

My previous work studies reliable visual reasoning under sparse,
corrupted, and distribution-shifted observations, including
monocular metric depth, anomaly detection,
and egocentric hand--object perception.
I am interested in connecting these perceptual capabilities with
robot policies that must \textbf{perceive reliably, retain information over time,
and act under changing real-world conditions}.

% ---------- Selected Research Projects ----------
\section{Selected Research Projects}

\resumeSubHeadingListStart

\resumeSubItem
  {MemoryPi --- Memory-Augmented Vision--Language--Action Model}
  {Investigating explicit long-term memory for VLA policies.
  The policy stores and retrieves task-relevant information across
  subtasks so that actions can depend on observations or outcomes
  no longer visible in the current frame. Motivated by long-horizon
  failure modes observed in RoboMME-style manipulation tasks.}

\resumeSubItem
  {Video-to-Simulation for Robot Learning}
  {Building a pipeline that uses computer-vision models to recover
  object geometry, hand--object interaction, and demonstrations
  from monocular human video, then retargets the reconstructed interaction
  into a robot simulation environment for learning and evaluation.}

\resumeSubItem
  {Generative Evaluation for VLA Policies}
  {Exploring action-conditioned robot video generation and learned
  world models as a complementary evaluation signal for scenes that
  are difficult to reproduce with manually authored 3D assets.
  Current focus is understanding when predicted visual futures are
  sufficiently reliable to evaluate robot behavior.}
